Heard, not [listened](/tsost/course/concepts/listening/) to.

> *"Furniture Music is fundamentally industrial. [...] A form of music designed to satisfy 'utility' requirements. [...] Furniture Music creates vibration; it has no other purpose; it fills the same role as light, warmth, and comfort in all its forms."*
> — Erik Satie, letter to Jean Cocteau, March 1920

Satie imagined music that could blend into the room, soften the sounds of knives and forks, fill awkward silences, and neutralize street noise.

- Satie's best-known pieces: [Gymnopédies & Gnossiennes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wnacdOIoTBQ&list=RDwnacdOIoTBQ&start_radio=1) (1888)
- Furniture music pieces: [Satie Furniture Music](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLX9xb_veUJ3qxPWs8cExBx7wEKx-6mhHF) (~1917)

Furniture music is the ancestor of most background music we live with now. Muzak. Ambient. The playlist in the café.

## Muzak

Muzak, a brand founded in 1934, produced music designed to shape behavior in workplaces, shops, and lifts. Usually called "Elevator Music".

- [What is Muzak? (Elevator Music)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=47mYs1SGBq4&t=117s)

## Ambient music

Brian Eno coined the term *Ambient Music* in 1978 with *Ambient 1: Music for Airports*.

> *"Ambient Music must be able to accommodate many levels of listening attention without enforcing one in particular; it must be as ignorable as it is interesting."*
> — Brian Eno, liner notes, *Ambient 1: Music for Airports*, 1978

- [Brian Eno — Music for Airports](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNwYtllyt3Q)
