## A

**Audiocumentary** — an audio documentary. A short recorded piece that captures the sonic character of a place or situation. The form used in [Assignment 1](/tsost/course/assignments/assignment-1/).

**Actuator** — a device that converts an electrical signal into physical action. In this course: a speaker, a motor, an LED.

**Amplitude** — the magnitude of a sound wave's displacement from equilibrium. Perceived as *loudness*.

**Attack** — the time it takes for a sound to reach its peak amplitude after onset. A piano has a sharp attack, a bowed string a slow one.

**Behavior** — the rule or logic that determines how a system responds to its environment.

## C

**Cross-modal description** — the use of vocabulary from other sensory domains (visual, tactile) to describe sound. "Warm," "sharp," "dark," and "rough" are all cross-modal. Common because sound lacks a dedicated descriptive vocabulary of its own.

## D

**[Decentralized system](/tsost/course/concepts/decentralized-systems/)** — a system with no central controller, where global behavior emerges from local interactions between nodes.

**Duration** — how long a sound lasts. One of the primary physical dimensions of sound, alongside frequency, amplitude, timbre, and space.

## E

**Ecological event perception** — the process by which listeners identify real-world sound sources and events from acoustic information alone. Central to how we make sense of our everyday soundscape.

**Emergence** — the appearance of complex, unpredictable behavior from the interaction of many simple components — none of which, alone, produces that behavior.

**Envelope** — the shape of a sound's amplitude over time. Classically described as: attack, decay, sustain, release (ADSR).

**Expressive sound** — sound that communicates through its own qualities — texture, rhythm, dynamics, register — rather than through learned association. The meaning is felt, not decoded. See also *Symbolic sound*.

## F

**[Field recording](/tsost/course/concepts/field-recording/)** — the capture of sound in real environments. Used in this course as a listening practice: a way of attending to the sonic character of a space before designing within it.

**Frequency** — the number of oscillation cycles per second, measured in Hertz (Hz). Perceived as *pitch*.

**[Furniture music](/tsost/course/concepts/furniture-music/)** — *musique d'ameublement*. Erik Satie's concept (1920) of music designed to be part of the environment — to be heard, not listened to. The conceptual frame for the Lucid.Bar final event.

## H

**Hearing** — the passive physiological reception of sound waves by the auditory system.

**Interaction** — the exchange between a person and an object: action produces response.

## L

**Latency** — the delay between an input event and its corresponding output. Critical in interactive and networked sound systems.

**[Listening](/tsost/course/concepts/listening/)** — the active, intentional direction of attention toward sound.

## M

**[Mapping](/tsost/course/concepts/mapping/)** — the design decision connecting sensor input parameters to sound output parameters.

**Microcontroller** — a small, self-contained programmable chip that reads sensors and controls outputs. In this course: the ESP32.

## N

**Node** — in a decentralized system, an autonomous unit that acts on local information and communicates with its neighbours. Each Smart Thing in the Lucid.Bar is a node.

## O

**Object** — a physical artifact with material properties, a history of use, and a position in space.

**[OSC](/tsost/course/tools/osc/)** — Open Sound Control. A network protocol for real-time communication between sound and multimedia devices.

## P

**Patch** — in [Max](/tsost/course/tools/max/) or [Plugdata](/tsost/course/tools/plugdata/), a visual program made of connected objects. The basic unit of work.

**[Psychoacoustics](/tsost/course/concepts/psychoacoustics/)** — the study of the relationship between physical sound properties and subjective auditory perception.

## R

**Reduced listening** — a mode of listening (Pierre Schaeffer) that attends to sound as pure sonic material, detached from its source or meaning.

**Reverberation** — the persistence of sound in a space after the source has stopped, caused by multiple reflections off surfaces. Shapes the perceived character of a space.

**Rule set** — the practical form of a behavior: if sensor reads X, output sound Y. The design space between sensing and sounding.

## S

**[Sensor](/tsost/course/tools/sensors/)** — a transducer that converts a physical phenomenon (motion, pressure, light, temperature...) into an electrical signal or digital data.

**Smart Thing** — an object augmented with a sensor and a sound behavior. The central artifact of this course.

**[Sound Design](/tsost/course/concepts/sound-design/)** — the practice of designing and shaping sound purposefully for a given context.

**Soundscape** — the acoustic character of a place — all the sounds that constitute it, heard together in context.

**Symbolic sound** — sound that carries learned, agreed meaning: alarms, notifications, status tones. The meaning is decoded, not felt. See also *Expressive sound*.

## T

**Thing** — an object that has been given presence beyond its function, through use, wear, and relationships with the people and space around it.

**[Thing-Centeredness](/tsost/course/concepts/thing-centeredness/)** — a design approach that takes the object as its starting point. Rather than asking what the user needs, it asks what the character of the object is, and what would extend — rather than overwrite — that character.

**Timbre** — the perceptual quality that distinguishes two sounds at the same pitch and loudness. Determined by harmonic spectrum and temporal envelope.

**Transducer** — any device that converts energy from one form to another. Microphones and speakers are both transducers.
